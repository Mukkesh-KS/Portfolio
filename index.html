0x00 — ABOUT

Field notes

I'm an Electronics and Communication Engineering student who ended up spending most of my time at the bench rather than in the lecture hall — schematic capture, PCB layout, and the firmware that has to make sense of whatever the hardware actually does, not what the datasheet promised.

Most of my hands-on experience comes from building end-to-end embedded systems: designing a board in KiCad, bringing it up when it inevitably doesn't work the first time, and then writing the drivers and communication stacks that turn a bare PCB into a working data acquisition or control system.

I'm drawn to the failure-mode end of firmware — the overvoltage fault that shouldn't be possible, the CAN bus that goes quiet for no obvious reason — and to the discipline of building things that keep working after they leave the bench.

0x01 — EXPERIENCE

Firmware intern

Firmware Intern — Welkinrim Technologies

May 2026 – Aug 2026

Built the application firmware for a custom STM32F767-based dual-motor thrust stand — software-configurable PWM generation for two motors over a percentage-based throttle interface, plus a USB-CDC virtual COM link for real-time control from a companion app, automating what used to be manual ESC calibration.

Led physical board bring-up on the first prototype run, salvaging it with track cuts and jumper wires rather than a costly respin. Traced a fault that was silently threatening to kill the MCU back to an overvoltage misrouting on the STM32's VCAP2 pin, and cleared a run of CAN, LDO, and USB power/data faults along the way.

Designed a deterministic RS485 data-logging pipeline sampling thrust, torque, ERPM, current, voltage, vibration, and temperature at 10 Hz across 8 channels, timestamped by a battery-backed RTC and streamed to onboard SD storage — sitting on top of a unified stack spanning 3 CAN buses, SPI, I2C, USB-C, Bluetooth, and Wi-Fi.
